Scope the proposal through real examples

Start with one architecture contract, then use seven reviewed 2023 projects as evidence about closed loops, stateful products, and scope.

30 minutes Proposal due Aug 4 Seven identifiable projects Student-safe summary Architecture → evidence → lesson

What seven reviewed projects showed[updated]

Everyone reached Kafka. Two closed the loop.

Held up

The front half

All seven built a working producer with a schema. Getting events into Kafka was not the hard part for anyone.

Broke

The same middle

Where projects failed, they failed at the consumer between Kafka and the store. Four of seven never finished it.

Blurred

The word "streaming"

It meant a live feed, a generator, a replayed archive, or a nightly job. All four are fine. Only some were declared.

Overstated

The diagram

Architecture diagrams were consistently larger than the delivered system. The grade followed what ran.

The pattern to avoid is specific. It is not "too ambitious" in general. It is a finished producer, a finished dashboard, and nothing that reliably connects them.

What to do differently, in your proposal[updated]

Five decisions to make before Tuesday

  1. Name the source pattern. Live feed, synthetic generator, replayed archive, or scheduled batch. Any of the four is acceptable; guessing which one you have is not.
  2. Build the middle first. Write the consumer that lands data in a store before you build the dashboard on top of it.
  3. Pick one stateful operation. One join, one window, or one current-state table. One is enough to demonstrate the course.
  4. Decide the evidence now. A screenshot, a log, or a test that proves it ran end to end, from the code you submit.
  5. Cut a layer, not the middle. Dropping the warehouse is fine. Leaving the consumer unfinished is not.
Every one of these came from a specific project you are about to see.

Proposal scope gate[updated]

Every proposal needs one complete, inspectable path

Proposal test: name the owner and proof for every arrow. If the middle cannot be built by the final deadline, cut scope now.

Project 1 of 7 · live source · closed loop[updated]

Transit arrival service

Goal. Give a rider the next train, the current delay, and a station map without opening the transit operator's own app.

Lesson. The most convincing project in the group was also one of the smallest. Every arrow had an owner, and the ideas that were not built were labelled future work instead of drawn as if finished.

Project 2 of 7 · live source · real stateful processing[updated]

Market anomaly detection

Goal. Flag one-minute trading windows whose volume and average price look abnormal against recent behaviour.

Lesson. This is Lecture 7B in production. The window and the watermark did more work than the model did, and the run output was kept as evidence.

Project 3 of 7 · scheduled batch · broadest scope[updated]

Civic request analytics

Goal. Show where city service requests cluster, and predict how long a new request will take to close.

Lesson. Call a daily pull what it is: batch ingestion replayed through Kafka. And the consumer in the middle has to run from the submitted code, not only in the recorded demo.

Project 4 of 7 · synthetic source · largest surface[updated]

Listening behaviour analytics

Goal. Analyse listening, login and page-view behaviour for a music service at a scale the team could control.

Lesson. Synthetic events are a legitimate way to obtain a stream, and reusing an open-source pipeline is allowed. What is required is saying plainly which parts you wrote.

Project 5 of 7 · replayed history · modules without the bridge[updated]

Transaction anomaly system

Goal. Separate suspicious transactions from normal ones, store each group, and monitor the split.

Lesson. Component count is not completion. The one piece that would have turned these modules into a system is the piece left as a placeholder.

Project 6 of 7 · replayed history · strong first half[updated]

Taxi operations analytics

Goal. Track trips, passenger counts, tips, vendors and airport activity as a live operations view.

Lesson. Getting data into Kafka and aggregating it is the part this course already taught you. The project is judged on what happens after that.

Project 7 of 7 · live source · smallest system[updated]

Live price chart

Goal. Watch one ticker's price and volume update live while the market is open.

Lesson. A small system that runs beats a large one that does not. Anomaly detection, storage, news matching and BI were all proposed and dropped, and the chart still worked.

July 30 classroom Q&A[updated]

The hard choices were source, scope, and the middle

Choose the source honestly

Live, replay, or synthetic

Past teams spent substantial time finding a usable data source and sometimes changed direction after testing the first tool.

Choose one technical risk

Complexity consumes the deadline

Spark, a warehouse, a model, and a dashboard can each be reasonable. Building all of them together can leave the connecting path late or incomplete.

Choose transferable reasoning

Tools change; responsibilities remain

Explain why Kafka, a store, or a processing owner is needed. Product syntax matters less than a complete, defensible data path.

Proposal choice: emphasize a producer and live source, a controlled replay or simulator, or downstream analytics and storage. Whichever focus you choose, still demonstrate one complete path from event to inspectable result.

Not every source was truly live[updated]

“Streaming” described several different source patterns

PatternSeen inWhat to say in your proposal
Live external eventsProjects 1, 2, 7A real-time source, with the polling interval or websocket boundary stated
Synthetic event streamProject 4Simulated events, generated to exercise a streaming system
Historical replayProjects 5, 6Archived data replayed as events, which is not a live source
Scheduled batch through KafkaProject 3Batch ingestion plus Kafka transport, not continuous generation
Precise language strengthens the project. Historical replay is completely acceptable when the student explains what is simulated and what is genuinely continuous.

Review every proposal the same way[updated]

Five questions reveal the real project

  1. Problem: who uses the result, and what decision changes?
  2. Events: what arrives, at what rate, and what is real versus replayed?
  3. Kafka: what are the topic, key, contract, retention, and replay reason?
  4. State: what join, window, aggregate, dedupe, or current answer must be maintained?
  5. Evidence: what output, metric, failure case, and fallback will be demonstrated?

Part 1 takeaway[updated]

Do less. Close the loop. Show the evidence.

One sourceReal, synthetic, or replay—named honestly
→
One Kafka pathKey, contract, and retained events
→
One meaningful operationState, time, or transformation
→
One inspectable resultPlus failure and replay evidence
The 2023 projects are valuable because they show both the ambition of real products and the cost of leaving the middle unfinished. Your project does not need every layer; it does need one end-to-end path that runs and can be explained.
