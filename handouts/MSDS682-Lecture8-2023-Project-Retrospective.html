Proposal scope gate

Every proposal needs one complete, inspectable path

Proposal test: name the owner and proof for every arrow. If the middle cannot be built by the final deadline, cut scope now.

Project 1 of 7 · live source · closed loop

Transit arrival service

Problem. A rider wants the next train and the current delay without opening the transit operator's own app.

Lesson. The most convincing project in the group was also one of the smallest. Every arrow had an owner, and the ideas that were not built were labelled future work instead of drawn as if finished.

Project 2 of 7 · live source · real stateful processing

Market anomaly detection

Problem. A trader wants to know which one-minute windows had abnormal volume and price while the market is open.

Lesson. This is Lecture 7B in production. The window and the watermark did more work than the model did, and the run output was kept as evidence.

Project 3 of 7 · scheduled batch · broadest scope

Civic request analytics

Problem. A city wants to see where service requests cluster and how long a new request will take to close.

Lesson. Call a daily pull what it is: batch ingestion replayed through Kafka. And the consumer in the middle has to run from the submitted code, not only in the recorded demo.

Project 4 of 7 · synthetic source · largest surface

Listening behaviour analytics

Problem. A music service wants listening, login, and page-view analytics at a volume the team can control.

Lesson. Synthetic events are a legitimate way to obtain a stream, and reusing an open-source pipeline is allowed. What is required is saying plainly which parts you wrote.

Project 5 of 7 · replayed history · modules without the bridge

Transaction anomaly system

Problem. A payments team wants suspicious transactions separated from normal ones, stored, and monitored.

Lesson. Component count is not completion. The one piece that would have turned these modules into a system is the piece left as a placeholder.

Project 6 of 7 · replayed history · strong first half

Taxi operations analytics

Problem. An operations team wants a live view of trips, passenger counts, tips, vendors, and airport activity.

Lesson. Getting data into Kafka and aggregating it is the part this course already taught you. The project is judged on what happens after that.

Project 7 of 7 · live source · smallest system

Live price chart

Problem. A trader wants one ticker's price and volume updating live while the market is open.

Lesson. A small system that runs beats a large one that does not. Anomaly detection, storage, news matching and BI were all proposed and dropped, and the chart still worked.

Not every source was truly live

“Streaming” described several different source patterns

PatternSeen inWhat to say in your proposal
Live external eventsProjects 1, 2, 7A real-time source, with the polling interval or websocket boundary stated
Synthetic event streamProject 4Simulated events, generated to exercise a streaming system
Historical replayProjects 5, 6Archived data replayed as events, which is not a live source
Scheduled batch through KafkaProject 3Batch ingestion plus Kafka transport, not continuous generation
Precise language strengthens the project. Historical replay is completely acceptable when the student explains what is simulated and what is genuinely continuous.

Review every proposal the same way

Five questions reveal the real project

  1. Problem: who uses the result, and what decision changes?
  2. Events: what arrives, at what rate, and what is real versus replayed?
  3. Kafka: what are the topic, key, contract, retention, and replay reason?
  4. State: what join, window, aggregate, dedupe, or current answer must be maintained?
  5. Evidence: what output, metric, failure case, and fallback will be demonstrated?

Part 1 takeaway

Do less. Close the loop. Show the evidence.

One sourceReal, synthetic, or replay, named honestly
→
One Kafka pathKey, contract, and retained events
→
One meaningful operationState, time, or transformation
→
One inspectable resultPlus failure and replay evidence
Cut a layer, not the middle. Dropping the warehouse is fine; leaving the consumer unfinished is what cost four of these seven projects their closed loop.
